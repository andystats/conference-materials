Object-Centric Causal Discovery in MIMIC-IV

A discovery-to-inference workflow for identifying candidate upstream drivers of hospital-acquired pressure injury.

Flat tables lose clinical time. Classic discovery can return an equivalence class instead of one oriented graph.
Patient objects keep clocks attached. Admissions, labs, medications, procedures, and outcomes stay in temporal context.
Discovery becomes a triage step. Candidate edges move to clinician review and confirmatory causal estimation.

Poster story

What the poster should leave people remembering

The site now follows the simplified poster rather than trying to be a full methods appendix. It keeps the core problem, the object-analytics move, and the HAPrI example.

1 · The stall

Classic causal discovery can identify ambiguity correctly.

When EHR data are flattened into one row per patient, clinically different stories can imply the same conditional-independence pattern. The honest output is then a CPDAG: an equivalence class with edges that remain unoriented.

Poster wording: flat tables ask the algorithm to recover clinical time after we have thrown clinical time away.

2 · The move

Object structure turns time into a search constraint.

Admissions, labs, medications, procedures, microbiology, transfers, and outcomes remain attached to the patient object. Temporal precedence can rule out backwards event edges and define the pre-index feature window.

Technical guardrail: temporality constrains orientation. It does not remove confounding or estimate an intervention effect.

3 · The case

In MIMIC-IV, hypoalbuminemia was prioritized as a candidate upstream driver of HAPrI.

In adult ICU admissions from MIMIC-IV v3.1, the object-centric search rediscovered known pressure-injury risk factors and surfaced albumin-related timing as a candidate signal worth clinical review.

Claim boundary: this is hypothesis-generating discovery, not proof that albumin management prevents pressure injury.

Key claims

Streamlined companion takeaways

Problem

Flat extracts hide ordering, relational context, and target windows that matter for causal orientation.

Method

Object-centric discovery searches over temporally ordered patient objects rather than detached feature columns.

Handoff

Candidate edges become prespecified estimands for clinician review, target-trial emulation, TMLE, or longitudinal g-methods.

Abstract in one paragraph

Clinical knowledge disciplines the search; discovery surfaces hypotheses.

Traditional causal discovery methods often assume flat data and return equivalence classes when observational evidence cannot orient all edges. We represent MIMIC-IV adult ICU admissions as temporally ordered patient objects and use temporal precedence, relational structure, and pre-index windows to constrain discovery for hospital-acquired pressure injury. The search prioritized hypoalbuminemia as a candidate upstream driver, rediscovered established risk factors, and flagged albumin/fluid timing for follow-up. The result is a prioritization workflow for confirmatory clinician review and causal estimation, not a final intervention claim.
